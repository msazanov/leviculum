# The most beautiful map a text terminal can show (#474)

Draft, 2026-10-28 (1090). Lew uses loquax's braille map over mosh from
Termux and asks what text mode can do beyond it. This draft compares the
cell techniques on the same views, says what cartography in cells needs,
which glyphs fonts actually carry, what each technique costs, and
recommends one default per situation.

**Status (1093, 2026-10-29): built** for slices 1 to 4 of section 5:
`braille-thin`, the 16-colour class tokens, octants and `blocks` as rungs
with the faster octant fit, and the glyph test behind `g`; plus a key that
cycles every way live and a memory of the one the reader stays on, per
terminal context (`loquax(1)`, MAP; `loquax-map.md`, section 6). The
encoders moved out of the sample generator into the map core,
`leviculum_map::cells`, which the generator now calls. Slices 5 (a dark
style table) and 6 (labels) are not built. The two-colour fit compares
its splits exactly now (a fraction, no integer division), so a sample
generated again may differ from the committed ones in a near tie.

Everything below was measured on the coder host (schneckenschreck, AMD
Ryzen 9 7950X) unless it says otherwise. Nothing was checked on Lew's
phone: the Termux findings are font tables and source, not a screen.

## Recommendation

1. **Octants are the most beautiful text map**: 2 x 4 pixels a cell, two
   colours each, every area filled and every road in its colour
   (`octant-*.ans`). A cell technique cannot do better in principle: a
   cell has two colours, and octants give the finest grid of them that
   Unicode has.
2. **Local truecolor, no image protocol**: octants where the terminal
   draws them itself (kitty, foot, WezTerm, recognised by `TERM` or
   `TERM_PROGRAM`), else `blocks-best` (quadrants plus eighth blocks,
   chosen per cell): the set every tested mono font but two carries.
   kitty or sixel stay on top where the terminal answers for one.
3. **Over mosh**: braille with one-dot lines in colour on cell background
   colours (`braille-thin`). Background colour needs no glyph, braille is
   the one family Termux's default font chain draws, and mosh passes
   truecolor (1088). Octants over mosh work when the client font has them.
4. **16 colours**: the same `braille-thin`, its colours chosen per class
   from the sixteen (`-16tok`), never the nearest of the sixteen, which
   turns parks and the river into grey (`-16` vs `-16tok`).
5. **No special glyphs**: one colour a cell as a background (`mosaic`),
   labels in text. Without colour either, braille stays the floor.
6. **Labels need data the region file does not carry**: place, street and
   water names are dropped at the cut (`leviculum-map-tool/src/mvt.rs:45`).
   A label table per tile is the first cartography slice.
7. **A test pattern** (`glyph-test.ans`, one key in the map) lets the
   reader see which sets their font draws; a program cannot ask.
8. **Cost is not the constraint**: at 155 x 65 cells the slowest encoder
   (octants, brute force) takes 34 ms, and fitting on the eight octant
   means instead of 32 pixels should cut that to about a quarter (not
   measured); all others stay under 5 ms. Bytes are: a colour frame is 60 KB at 80 x 24.

built only with `--features picture`, in no package) from the Frankfurt

Each file is `cat`-able, its last row a caption naming technique, view,
colour tier and size.

- Committed, 1.3 MB: `docs/drafts/terminal-map-samples/`.
  `samples/<technique>-frankfurt-z13.ans` for all twelve techniques,
  `all.ans` (the contact sheet of those twelve with titles),
  `glyph-test.ans`, and a dozen others: octant and braille-thin at
  `europe-z3` and `berlin-z15`, `blocks-best-berlin-z11`, and the colour
  tier and dark variants of frankfurt-z13 (`-256`, `-16`, `-16tok`,
  `-dark`).
- Not committed, 24 MB: `/home/lew/scratch/1090/out/` on schneckenschreck.
  Every technique at every view (frankfurt and berlin at z11, z13, z15,
  `germany-z5`, `europe-z3`) at 80 x 24 and 155 x 65, the tier and dark
  variants of the featured five at every view, `all.ans` over every view
  at 80 x 24 (2.6 MB), `costs.txt` and `render-native.txt`.

How a view is drawn: once, by the map core through
`loquax::picture::Painter`, into a source frame of 4 x 8 pixels a cell, one
zoom deeper than the view's name and at twice the line width
(`percent = 200`). That is today's braille density at the named zoom on
a finer grain, so every technique encodes the same frame and the samples
differ by encoding alone. The line widths are in the same band of
`leviculum_map::profile::line_widths` at z and z+1 for every view used.
Pins are placed with `leviculum_map::pins::labels`, the core's label
placement, as loquax places them.

`germany-z5` is all land at 80 x 24: at braille density z5 an 80 cell
view is 7 degrees wide, inside Germany, so the outline draws nothing but
land. `europe-z3` is the outline view that shows coasts.

## 1. The cell techniques

| technique | glyphs | grid | colours a cell | what it shows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `braille-mono` (today) | U+2800 | 2 x 4 dots | terminal default | lines only |
| `braille-colour` | U+2800 | 2 x 4 dots | foreground | lines in their class colour |
| `braille-bg` | U+2800 | 2 x 4 dots | fg + bg | areas as cell background, lines as dots |
| `braille-thin` | U+2800 | 2 x 4 dots | fg + bg | as `braille-bg`, lines one dot wide |
| `half` (today's half-blocks) | `▀` | 1 x 2 | fg + bg | everything, coarse |
| `quadrant` | U+2596..259F | 2 x 2 | fg + bg | everything |
| `sextant` | U+1FB00..1FB3B | 2 x 3 | fg + bg | everything |
| `octant` | U+1CD00..1CDE5 | 2 x 4 | fg + bg | everything, finest |
| `blocks-best` | quadrants + `▁▂▃▅▆▇▔▎▊` | best fit | fg + bg | everything, chafa style |
| `all-best` | blocks + sextants + octants | best fit | fg + bg | everything, chafa style |
| `box-hybrid` | box drawing, braille | edge joins | fg + bg | areas as background, roads as `─┼━` |
| `mosaic` | space | 1 x 1 | bg | one pixel a cell |

The two-colour techniques use one encoder: for each cell, every glyph of
the set as a mask over the cell's 32 source pixels, the two colours the
means of the pixels under and outside it, and the glyph with the least
squared error wins (what chafa does). `half`, `quadrant` and `octant`
are exact on the 4 x 8 grid; sextant rows are 3, 3 and 2 source pixels.
The braille techniques set a dot where any of its 2 x 2 source pixels is
a line (from an `Ink::Lines` draw), colour the dots with the commonest
line colour in the cell and the background with the commonest area
colour. White minor roads are grey as dots (`braille_ink`): a dot is a
few pixels of a glyph and white vanishes on the light ground.

What the samples show:

- **`octant` and `all-best`** look the same at 80 x 24 (all-best picks an
  octant wherever it fits better, so it is octants plus a few eighths).
  Roads read as roads with their widths, the Main as a river, parks and
  buildings as areas. At 155 x 65 it is a map you would print.
- **`blocks-best`** comes close: diagonals step in quadrants (2 x 2)
  where octants step in 2 x 4, horizontal edges hit eighths. Visibly
  better than `half`.
- **`half`** is right and blurry: 1 x 2 a cell.
- **`sextant`** sits between quadrants and octants, its 3-row grid
  slightly uneven on an 8-row cell.
- **braille with background** reads very differently: areas are blocky
  (one colour a cell), lines are crisp dots. With today's line widths
  (`braille-bg`) a minor road at z13 is two dots wide and the city turns
  into `⣿`; one-dot lines (`braille-thin`) keep the street grid legible.
  The look Lew likes, plus areas.
- **`box-hybrid`** is legible at z15 (roads as `━┃┏` in their colour) and
  noise below: a diagonal road is a staircase of `┘┌`. Not worth a rung;
  box drawing is for a route or a selected road, not for the street net.
- **`mosaic`** keeps areas and loses most roads (a road rarely is the
  commonest colour of a cell). A floor, not a choice.

## 2. Cartography in cells

**Names are missing at the source.** `leviculum-map-tool` keeps the
`landuse`, `water` and `roads` layers as geometry classes and nothing
else (`leviculum-map-tool/src/mvt.rs:45` to `:65`); the Protomaps
`places` and `pois` layers and every feature's `name` are dropped, and
`leviculum-map` says "text is never drawn here: labels are data a
frontend places" (`leviculum-map/src/lib.rs:22`). The samples therefore
carry pins (the core's placement) and no street names.

What a label table needs, per tile: points (place name, kind,
population rank; POI name and kind) and line labels (road name, class,
an anchor point and a direction from the longest straight run in the
tile). In cells:

- **Places** first, by rank, one name per 20 x 6 cells at most at
  80 x 24; a place's name beside its marker, placed by
  `pins::labels` with pins taking precedence (they are placed first and
  nothing covers them).
- **Roads** at z15 and deeper only, major roads first, the name written
  along a horizontal or vertical run of at least its length plus two
  cells, never along a diagonal (a terminal cannot rotate text, and a
  staircase of letters does not read). Dropped where it would cross a
  pin or another label.
- **Water and park names** in the area's colour family, dim, at z13 and
  deeper, at the area's widest horizontal run.
- **Symbols** for POIs as plain Unicode first, from the Geometric Shapes
  block: `●` pin (taken), `◉` selected, `▲` peak, `■` building of note.
  All four are in every tested mono font but Liberation Mono (no `◉`) and
  Droid Sans Mono (none; Noto Sans Symbols 2 has all four). Emoji such as `☕` or `⚓` are wide in some terminals and break
  the cell grid, so they are left out; Nerd Font glyphs only behind an
  option, since nothing reports whether the font has them.
- **Label style**: dark text on white at light ground, light text on
  near black in dark (`pin` in the generator); one cell of the
  map colour around a label is not possible in cells, so the label box
  itself is the halo.

**What to drop per zoom at 80 x 24**, proposed after looking at the
samples (the zoom bands of `line_widths` already drop most of it):

| zoom | keep | drop |
|---|---|---|
| z3 to z6 | coasts, water areas, place names of rank 1 to 3, pins | everything else |
| z7 to z10 | major roads, rail, large parks, cities | minor roads, paths, buildings |
| z11, z12 | minor roads as one dot (braille) or one sub-pixel row | paths |
| z13, z14 | paths, all areas; road names of major roads | buildings in braille |
| z15+ | everything, street names | nothing |

**Colour tiers.** The style is mapfill's light table. On a dark terminal
the colour techniques bring their own ground, so the light map stays
legible, but a light rectangle in a dark UI is loud; the `-dark` samples
remap the table one colour for one colour (`darken`), and that reads
well. In 256 colours the nearest index keeps every class apart
(`-256`). In 16 colours the nearest of the sixteen does not: park,
earth and water all land on white or grey. Choosing the index per class
(`tokens16`: earth white, park green, water bright blue, major roads
yellow, buildings grey) restores the map (`-16tok`). That is 1042's rule
for the palette (`leviculum_ui::color`, a 16 or 256 token names an index
itself "because the nearest index does not keep contrast") applied to
the map's classes: the style needs a 16-colour token per class, as data
beside the RGB table.

## 3. Robustness: which glyphs fonts carry

A program cannot ask the terminal whether its font has a glyph. Measured
from the fonts' cmap tables (fontTools, script in
`/home/lew/scratch/1090/tools/coverage.py`); counts are glyphs present out
of the range:

| font (version) | braille | `▀▄█` | quadrants | eighths | box | sextants | octants |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DejaVu Sans Mono 2.37 | 0/256 | 3/3 | 10/10 | 15/15 | 128/128 | 0/60 | 0/230 |
| DejaVu Sans 2.37 (proportional) | 256/256 | 3/3 | 10/10 | 15/15 | 128/128 | 0/60 | 0/230 |
| Noto Sans Mono 2.006 | 0/256 | 3/3 | 10/10 | 15/15 | 128/128 | 0/60 | 0/230 |
| Hack 3.003 | 0/256 | 3/3 | 10/10 | 15/15 | 128/128 | 0/60 | 0/230 |
| JetBrains Mono 2.304 | 0/256 | 3/3 | 10/10 | 15/15 | 128/128 | 0/60 | 0/230 |
| Fira Code 6.002 | 0/256 | 3/3 | 10/10 | 15/15 | 128/128 | 0/60 | 0/230 |
| Cascadia Code 2407.024 | 256/256 | 3/3 | 10/10 | 15/15 | 128/128 | 60/60 | 230/230 |
| Liberation Mono 2.1.5 | 0/256 | 3/3 | 0/10 | 3/15 | 40/128 | 0/60 | 0/230 |
| Droid Sans Mono 1.00 (Android) | 0/256 | 0/3 | 0/10 | 0/15 | 0/128 | 0/60 | 0/230 |
| Roboto 2.138 (Android sans) | 0/256 | 0/3 | 0/10 | 0/15 | 0/128 | 0/60 | 0/230 |
| Noto Sans Symbols 2 2.003 | 256/256 | 0/3 | 0/10 | 0/15 | 0/128 | 60/60 | 0/230 |

Cascadia Code also has the 26 non-octant patterns the octant table
borrows (`U+1CEA0` quarter blocks, `U+1FB82`, `U+1FBE6`); DejaVu Sans
Mono has none of those.

So braille is never in the mono font: it always comes from fallback
(DejaVu Sans on a stock Debian, Noto Sans Symbols 2 on Android). That is
why it works almost everywhere, and why its dots can look a different
size from the text.

**Termux.** Termux draws with `Typeface.MONOSPACE` unless
`~/.termux/font.ttf` exists (termux-app,
`TermuxTerminalSessionActivityClient.java`, `checkForFontAndColors`), and
Android's `monospace` family is `DroidSansMono.ttf` (AOSP
`frameworks/base/data/fonts/fonts.xml`, family `monospace`). Droid Sans
Mono has none of the sets; Android's fallback chain then decides. Its
symbol fallback, `NotoSansSymbols-Regular-Subsetted2.ttf` (family
`und-Zsym`), is a subset of Noto Sans Symbols 2, which carries braille and
sextants but no block elements, and Roboto carries none either. That
fits Lew's screen: braille drew, `▀` came out as a box. A fit, not a
proof: the phone's fallback files were not read, and they differ by
Android version. A Termux user fixes it with one file:
`~/.termux/font.ttf` set to a font like Cascadia Code, after which every
technique works there. Termux's width table (`WcWidth.java`, Unicode 15)
gives every non-wide character width 1, so octants do not break its
layout even though they are Unicode 16.

**Terminals that draw the glyphs themselves**, so the font does not
matter: foot draws U+2500..259F, braille, octants and U+1FB00..1FB9B
unless `box-drawings-uses-font-glyphs` is set (`foot.ini(5)`); kitty
draws the same ranges and more (`kitty/fonts.c`, the `0x1cd00 ...
0x1cde5` octant case beside braille and legacy computing); WezTerm has
sextant, octant and braille patterns in `wezterm-gui/src/customglyph.rs`;
Alacritty's built-in font covers U+2500..259F and the sextants
U+1FB00..1FB3B but neither octants nor braille
(`alacritty/src/renderer/text/builtin_font.rs`). All from their sources
at their default branches on 2026-10-28, none run here.

**mosh.** mosh-server keeps a character only if the server's `wcwidth`
knows it (`src/terminal/terminal.cc`, `case -1: /* unprintable character
*/ break;`). glibc 2.41 (Debian 13, measured: width 1 for U+1CD00 and
U+1CDE5) knows octants; an older glibc with Unicode 15 does not, and the
server would drop every octant silently. So over mosh: braille and
blocks always arrive, octants only from a server with glibc 2.41 or later,
and what the client shows is the client font's business.

**The test pattern.** `glyph-test.ans` is one line per set (half blocks,
quadrants, eighths, braille, box drawing, sextants, octants). In loquax it
is one key in the map (proposed: `g`), drawn as an overlay, with the
`--graphics` value each line stands for, so the reader picks the rung
they see working and puts it in the config.

**How this composes with 1088's choice.** The automatic choice stays
facts only: `TERM` and `TERM_PROGRAM` name the terminals that draw
octants themselves (kitty `xterm-kitty`, foot `foot`, WezTerm
`TERM_PROGRAM=WezTerm`), mosh is found among the parents, the tier
comes from the colour ladder. Every other case gets the set the font
measurements say is safe, and the test pattern and the flag correct it.

## 4. Cost

Release build, median of five, from `costs.txt` and `render-native.txt`.
"Render" is the map core's draw; the samples' source frame is three draws
(colour, lines, thin lines) at 4 x 8 a cell, where loquax today draws once
at its rung's own density.

| | 80 x 24 | 155 x 65 |
|---|---|---|
| render today, half-blocks (1 x 2 at z) | 0.3 to 0.6 ms | 0.7 to 1.0 ms |
| render today, braille (2 x 4 at z, lines) | 0.5 to 0.8 ms | 2.0 to 2.3 ms |
| render, source frame (4 x 8, three draws) | 3.0 to 4.3 ms | 8.8 to 18.9 ms |

Encode plus ANSI, frankfurt-z13; bytes per full frame:

| technique | encode 80 x 24 | encode 155 x 65 | bytes truecolor 80 x 24 | 256 | 16 | cells changed by a one-cell pan |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| braille-mono | 0.08 ms | 0.43 ms | 5.9 KB | 5.9 KB | 5.9 KB | 77 % |
| braille-thin | 0.74 ms | 3.81 ms | 20.1 KB | 14.4 KB | 10.0 KB | 87 % |
| half | 0.37 ms | 1.80 ms | 60.9 KB | 37.5 KB | 20.2 KB | 92 % |
| blocks-best | 0.89 ms | 4.69 ms | 63.0 KB | 38.8 KB | 20.9 KB | 93 % |
| octant | 6.25 ms | 33.65 ms | 63.6 KB | 39.7 KB | 22.0 KB | 93 % |
| all-best | 7.10 ms | 38.32 ms | 63.3 KB | 39.4 KB | 21.6 KB | 93 % |
| box-hybrid | 0.67 ms | 3.68 ms | 18.5 KB | 13.0 KB | 8.9 KB | 83 % |
| mosaic | 0.22 ms | 1.29 ms | 14.5 KB | 9.3 KB | 5.6 KB | 35 % |

Reading:

- Every encoder but the brute-force octant fit is under 5 ms at 155 x 65.
  The octant fit tries 256 masks on 32 pixels a cell; on the eight
  octant means instead of 32 pixels it is a quarter, and a 2-means split
  of eight values needs no enumeration at all. Built in 1093 on the
  eight sums, half the patterns (a pattern and its complement fit alike)
  in Gray code order: 5.8 ms against 31 ms at 155 x 65 over the Florence
  fixture, the same cells as the brute force.
- Bytes: a colour frame is about 60 KB at 80 x 24 in truecolor, 300 KB at
  155 x 65, because almost every cell carries two SGR sequences. 256
  colours cost 60 %, 16 colours a third.
- mosh sends the difference between frames, not the program's bytes, and
  a pan changes 77 to 93 % of the cells: a pan over mosh is a full frame
  in any technique. Braille with background sends a third of the colour
  techniques' bytes, the strongest argument for it over a slow link
  besides the glyph.

## 5. Slices

1. *(built, 1093, as the rung over mosh, in Termux and at 16 colours;
   plain braille stays for no colour and as a choice)*
   **`braille-thin` replaces today's braille** under colour: areas as cell
   background in the style's colours, lines at half width (one dot),
   minor roads grey as dots. Over mosh and at 16 colours (with the class
   tokens of slice 2). `--no-color` keeps braille in the terminal's
   colour. Snapshot-tested like today's braille.
2. *(built, 1093: `leviculum_mapfill::style::ansi16`)*
   **16-colour class tokens** in the style table beside the RGB, and the
   256 tier left on nearest-index (measured to keep the classes apart).
3. *(built, 1093, the flag values `octant` and `blocks`)*
   **Octants and blocks-best as rungs** between images and braille:
   `--graphics octants|blocks`, auto octants on kitty, foot and WezTerm by
   `TERM`/`TERM_PROGRAM`, else blocks-best replacing half-blocks. The
   faster octant fit (means of eight) lands with it.
4. *(built, 1093, `g` in the map)*
   **The glyph test pattern** behind one key, naming the flag per line.
5. **A dark style table** as data (`darken`'s table made real), chosen by
   the terminal background query (OSC 11) where it answers, else by
   config.
6. **Labels**: a label table per tile in the region format (places, POIs,
   road and water names, as above), `leviculum-map-tool` cutting it from
   the Protomaps `places`, `pois` and `name` attributes, placement in the
   core next to `pins::labels`, shared with the GUI and the M9.

Box drawing stays out of the ladder; it comes back for drawing a route.
